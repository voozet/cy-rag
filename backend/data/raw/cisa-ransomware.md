section: Ransomware

CISA's #StopRansomware Guide describes ransomware as a type of malware designed to encrypt files on a device or network, rendering them and the systems that rely on them unusable, after which the attacker demands a ransom payment in exchange for decryption. Many modern ransomware operations also exfiltrate data before encryption and threaten to publish or sell it, a tactic CISA refers to as double extortion, which is used to pressure victims into paying even if they can restore from backups. CISA outlines recommended prevention practices including maintaining offline, encrypted backups of critical data and regularly testing the restoration process; keeping operating systems, software, and firmware patched and up to date; enforcing phishing-resistant multi-factor authentication, particularly for remote access services such as VPNs and RDP; disabling unused ports and protocols, especially RDP if it is not required; and applying the principle of least privilege to limit the ability of a compromised account to move laterally or access sensitive systems. For response, CISA recommends that organizations have a ransomware-specific incident response plan that designates roles and communication channels in advance, and advises against paying the ransom, noting that payment does not guarantee recovery of data, does not guarantee stolen data will not be leaked, and may encourage further attacks. If an incident occurs, CISA recommends isolating affected systems immediately to prevent further spread, preserving evidence such as ransom notes and logs for investigation, and reporting the incident to CISA and law enforcement such as the FBI, who can provide assistance and may have decryption tools available for some ransomware families through resources such as the No More Ransom project.
