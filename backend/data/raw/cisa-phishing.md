section: Phishing
---
CISA describes phishing as a form of social engineering in which an attacker sends a fraudulent message designed to trick a person into revealing sensitive information, transferring money, or installing malicious software. Phishing is typically delivered by email, but CISA notes related variants including smishing (phishing via SMS text message) and vishing (phishing via voice call), as well as spear phishing, which targets a specific individual or organization using information gathered about the target to make the message more convincing. Common phishing lures described by CISA include messages that impersonate a trusted brand, colleague, or government agency; create a false sense of urgency (such as claiming an account will be suspended); or offer something enticing to the recipient. CISA's recommended defensive practices span both people and technology. On the human side, organizations should provide regular security awareness training so users can recognize suspicious sender addresses, mismatched or shortened links, unexpected attachments, and requests for credentials or payment, and should establish a simple mechanism for employees to report suspected phishing messages. On the technical side, CISA recommends deploying phishing-resistant multi-factor authentication so that a stolen password alone is insufficient for an attacker to gain access, implementing email authentication protocols such as SPF, DKIM, and DMARC to reduce spoofed messages, using web and email filtering to block known malicious domains and attachments, and keeping software patched to reduce the impact if a malicious link or attachment is opened. CISA also advises organizations to have an incident reporting and response process specifically for phishing, so that credentials entered on a phishing site can be reset quickly and any resulting account activity can be investigated, limiting the damage from successful phishing attempts.
