---
title: Digital Identity Guidelines — Authentication and Access Control (SP 800-63B)
source: NIST
url: https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-3/sp800-63b.html
section: Authenticator Assurance
---
NIST Special Publication 800-63B establishes technical requirements for authenticators used to verify a claimant's identity in digital systems, organized around three Authenticator Assurance Levels (AAL1, AAL2, AAL3) of increasing rigor. AAL1 requires single-factor authentication and provides some assurance that the claimant controls an authenticator bound to the account. AAL2 requires two distinct authentication factors, drawn from something you know (a password or PIN), something you have (a physical or software authenticator such as a hardware token or authenticator app), or something you are (a biometric), and is intended for use cases where compromise of a single factor would create meaningful risk. AAL3 requires a hardware-based authenticator and proof of possession of a cryptographic key through a protocol resistant to verifier impersonation, and is intended for the highest-risk scenarios. NIST notes that multi-factor authentication substantially reduces the risk from credential theft techniques such as phishing and credential stuffing, because a stolen password alone is insufficient for an attacker to authenticate. The guidance also discourages certain legacy practices: SP 800-63B recommends against mandatory periodic password rotation absent evidence of compromise (as this tends to produce weaker, more predictable passwords), and it deprecated SMS-based one-time codes as a sole second factor for higher assurance levels due to risks such as SIM-swapping and interception, recommending cryptographic or possession-based authenticators instead. On access control more broadly, the guidelines are complemented by the principle of least privilege, under which accounts and services should be granted only the permissions required to perform their function, reducing the impact if any single account or credential is compromised.
